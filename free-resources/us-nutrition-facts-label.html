How to read the US Nutrition Facts label

A practical five-point scan for the Nutrition Facts panel used on packaged foods in the United States. Start with the serving, then use the numbers to compare similar products.

1 · Check serving size and servings per container

The calories and nutrient amounts on the panel are usually shown for one serving. Check how many servings are in the package and compare the stated serving with the amount you actually eat.

Important: serving size reflects the amount people typically eat or drink. It is not a recommendation of how much you should eat.

2 · Look at calories per serving

Calories describe the energy in one serving. If you eat two servings, you generally consume twice the calories and nutrient amounts listed for one serving. Some packages show both per-serving and per-package information.

3 · Use the 5% and 20% Daily Value guide

As a general guide on the US label, 5% Daily Value (%DV) or less is low, while 20% DV or more is high for a nutrient in one serving.

Use %DV to understand how much one serving contributes to a daily reference amount. It is not a score for the whole food, and percentages for different nutrients are not added together.

4 · Notice nutrients to limit and nutrients to get enough of

The FDA highlights saturated fat, sodium and added sugars as nutrients to get less of. Dietary fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron and potassium are nutrients many people should get more of.

Compare similar products and consider the full label and your own dietary needs rather than judging a product from one number.

5 · Separate total sugars from added sugars

Total Sugars includes sugars naturally present in foods such as fruit and milk, as well as any added sugars. Added Sugars refers to sugars added during processing or preparation, including sugars from syrups and honey and certain concentrated juices.

Check both the Added Sugars line and the ingredient list. A sweet-tasting food is not explained by one number alone.

Remember: The Nutrition Facts panel tells you amounts and reference values. It does not provide a complete judgement of a food or replace advice for an allergy, medical condition or individual dietary need.
