Yes, and yours can have real butter in it. The only trick is the little holes, and a fork does that job.

How the factory makes Ritz crackers

The dough is rolled thin, cut with a scalloped round cutter and with small holes. The crackers are baked, then sprayed with oil and sprinkled with salt.Docked: Poked with small holes so steam can escape and the dough bakes flat.

  1. The dough is rolled thin
  2. A scalloped cutter shapes each cracker
  3. The crackers are docked with little holes
  4. They're baked, oiled and salted

The factory secret

🔒 What you can't easily copyThe holes aren't decoration. They let steam escape while baking. Without them, every cracker puffs up into a little pillow.

How to make Ritz crackers at home

  1. Mix flour, baking powder, a little sugar and salt, rub in cold butter, add water
  2. Roll thin, cut circles and poke holes with a fork or a straw
  3. Bake until golden, then brush with melted butter and sprinkle salt

Brush with butter while they're still hot, so it soaks in.

What the label tells you

US Ritz Original contains no butter. The fat is canola and palm oil, and the buttery taste comes from "natural flavor".

Questions people ask

Why do Ritz crackers have holes?

So steam escapes during baking and the crackers stay flat instead of puffing up.

Is there butter in Ritz crackers?

Not in US Ritz Original. They're made with canola and palm oil plus natural flavour.
