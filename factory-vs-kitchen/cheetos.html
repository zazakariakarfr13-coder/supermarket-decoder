Can You Make Cheetos at Home? 🧀

Sort of. The cheesy flavour is easy to copy, but the airy crunch comes from an extruder, a machine no home kitchen has. A baked cornmeal stick gets you most of the way.

How the factory makes Cheetos

Moist cornmeal is forced through an under heat and pressure. As it leaves the , the sudden drop in pressure turns the water inside to steam, and the snack puffs up in a fraction of a second. The pieces are cut, fried or baked, then tumbled in cheese seasoning.Extruder: A machine that squeezes dough through a shaped hole, like a giant Play-Doh press.Die: The shaped hole at the end of an extruder. It decides the snack's shape.

  1. Moist cornmeal goes into the machine
  2. It's forced through an extruder under heat and pressure
  3. Steam puffs it the instant it leaves the machine
  4. The pieces are cooked and tumbled in cheese powder

The factory secret

🔒 What you can't easily copyThe puff happens in a split second, when the pressure drops at the die. That instant expansion is what gives Cheetos their light, airy crunch.

How to make Cheetos at home

  1. Make a dough with cornmeal replacing part of the flourChoux: The dough used for eclairs and cream puffs. Steam puffs it up in the oven.
  2. Pipe thin sticks onto a tray and bake until fully crisp
  3. Toss in melted butter and cheese powder (the packet from boxed mac and cheese works)

The flavour is spot on. The crunch is denser, about 80% of the way there.

What the label tells you

That bright orange isn't only cheese. US Crunchy Cheetos list Yellow 6 among their colours (E110 in Europe). Recipes vary by flavour and country.

Questions people ask

How are Cheetos made?

Wet cornmeal is pushed through an extruder; the pressure drop at the exit puffs it with steam. Then it's cooked and coated in cheese seasoning.

Why are Cheetos so orange?

Cheese seasoning plus added colour: the US Crunchy cheese flavour lists Yellow 6.
