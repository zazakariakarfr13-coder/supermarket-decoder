Can You Make Nutella at Home? 🌰

Yes, and it's easier than most people think. Roasted hazelnuts blended long enough turn into butter; add cocoa, sugar and milk powder and you have a spread with far more hazelnut than the jar.

How the factory makes Nutella

The factory roasts hazelnuts and grinds them into a paste with sugar, vegetable oil, cocoa, skimmed milk powder, and vanillin. The mixture is then until the particles are too small to feel, which is where the silky texture comes from.Lecithin: An emulsifier, often from soy or sunflower, that keeps oil and other ingredients mixed.Roller-refined: Squeezed between steel rollers until the bits are too small to feel on your tongue.

  1. Hazelnuts are roasted and ground
  2. Sugar, oil, cocoa and milk powder are mixed in
  3. The paste is roller-refined until silky smooth

The factory secret

🔒 What you can't easily copyThe label tells the story. Sugar is the first ingredient and hazelnuts make up 13% of the jar. It's a sugar spread that tastes of hazelnut.

How to make Nutella at home

  1. Roast hazelnuts at 180°C for about 10 minutes, then rub off the skins in a towel
  2. Blend for 8-12 minutes, scraping the sides, until they turn into a runny butter
  3. Add cocoa, powdered sugar, milk powder, a pinch of salt and a splash of neutral oil

Yours will be slightly grainier, because a food processor can't grind as finely as factory rollers. It costs a bit more, since hazelnuts are pricey.

What the label tells you

Ingredients vary by country: some jars say palm oil, some say vegetable oil. Read the jar in your hand: sugar first, hazelnuts 13%.

Questions people ask

How much hazelnut is in Nutella?

13%, as printed on the label. Sugar is listed first, so it's the biggest ingredient by weight.

Is homemade Nutella healthier?

It usually has much more hazelnut and less sugar, because you choose the amounts. It's still a sweet spread.
