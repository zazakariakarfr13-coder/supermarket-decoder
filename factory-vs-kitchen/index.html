Factory vs. Kitchen

Can you make it at home?

How famous packaged foods are really made, the one factory secret you can't easily copy, and an honest verdict on whether your kitchen can do it.

How the verdicts work. Yes means a homemade version is as good or better. Sort of means you can get most of the way, but one factory trick is missing. No way means the machine or the secret recipe makes it impossible at home. The kitchen score (out of 10) is how close a careful home cook can get.

About the estimates: Kitchen scores are editorial judgments, not lab measurements. Store-price and home-cost figures are illustrative estimates that vary by country, retailer, date, recipe and yield. Brand names are used only to identify products; no endorsement is implied.

Factory words, explained

The words you'll meet on these pages, in plain English.

Alkaline
Made with mineral salts that make noodles springy, chewy and slightly yellow.
Baked baking soda
Baking soda heated in the oven for an hour. It becomes stronger, close to ramen salts.
Black cocoa
Cocoa treated with alkali until it's almost black. Darker colour, milder taste.
Canoe pan
A baking pan with long, narrow, rounded cups.
Carbonated
Water with carbon dioxide gas dissolved in it. That's the fizz.
Choux
The dough used for eclairs and cream puffs. Steam puffs it up in the oven.
Citric acid
A sour powder from citrus fruit. Mixed with baking soda and water, it fizzes.
Concentrate
The flavour base with most of the water left out. The water gets added later.
Die
The shaped hole at the end of an extruder. It decides the snack's shape.
Docked
Poked with small holes so steam can escape and the dough bakes flat.
Emulsifier
An ingredient that keeps oil and water-based ingredients from separating.
Enrobed
Fully coated by passing under a curtain of melted chocolate.
Extruder
A machine that squeezes dough through a shaped hole, like a giant Play-Doh press.
Kansui
Alkaline mineral water used in ramen noodles. It gives them their bounce.
Lecithin
An emulsifier, often from soy or sunflower, that keeps oil and other ingredients mixed.
Lentils
Small flat-round pieces, shaped like lentil beans.
Milled
Ground so finely that it becomes perfectly smooth.
Panned
Tumbled in a spinning drum and coated one thin layer at a time.
Panning
Coating sweets by tumbling them in a spinning drum, one thin layer at a time.
Psi
Pounds per square inch, a unit of pressure. A car tire holds about 32 psi.
Rework
Factory word for broken products that get recycled back into the recipe.
Roller-refined
Squeezed between steel rollers until the bits are too small to feel on your tongue.
Rotary molding
Pressing dough into a turning metal roller with the cookie shape carved in it.
Saddle
A curved chip shape: up on two sides, down on the other two. That's why the chips stack.
Sheeting line
Machines that roll dough into one long, perfectly even sheet.
Starch mogul
A machine that stamps candy shapes into trays of cornstarch, then fills them.
Tempered
Melted and cooled carefully so the chocolate sets shiny and snaps cleanly.
Trade secret
Business information kept secret instead of patented, so it never has to be published.
