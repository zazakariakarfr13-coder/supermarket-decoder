About the project

Understand the label. Keep the choice yours.

Supermarket Decoder turns food-label language into practical explanations people can use while shopping.

Who is behind it?

Supermarket Decoder is an independent educational project created by Zakaria Froud, a food-industry technician in training. The project approaches labels from the product and manufacturing side as well as the shopper's side: ingredients have functions, recipes are designed for specific purposes, and the label is one way to understand those choices.

The project does not present its author as a doctor, dietitian or food-law authority. Its role is to explain terminology and help readers ask better questions.

How the guidance is framed

  • Context before alarm. An additive name or E-number is not, by itself, proof that a food is harmful.
  • Read the whole label. Ingredients, quantities, serving sizes, allergens and the product's intended use all matter.
  • Separate processing from nutrition. A processing category is not a complete nutritional score or medical diagnosis.
  • Respect local rules. Labelling rules differ between countries. Examples from the EU, UK, US, Morocco and other markets must not be treated as one universal legal standard.
  • Keep shopping practical. The aim is to compare similar products and make informed choices within real budgets and routines.
Educational scope: The website and guide provide general information, not medical, dietetic or personalised nutrition advice. For allergies, medical conditions or individual dietary needs, speak with a qualified health professional.
