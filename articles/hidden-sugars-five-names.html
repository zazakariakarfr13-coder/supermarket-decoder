Hidden sugar names: 8 to spot on food labels

Sugar and sweetening ingredients do not always appear under one simple name. These examples are clues to understand, not proof that a food is unhealthy.

1 · Cane sugar

Sucrose made from sugar cane. When it is added to a packaged food, it contributes added sugars.

2 · Corn syrup

A syrup made from corn starch. It can add sweetness and help shape a product's texture.

3 · Glucose syrup

A starch-derived syrup containing glucose and related carbohydrates. Manufacturers use it for sweetness, moisture and texture.

4 · Dextrose

A form of glucose. It can be used for sweetness and for other formulation purposes.

5 · Invert sugar

A mixture of glucose and fructose made by breaking down sucrose. It adds sweetness and can help retain moisture.

6 · Honey

Honey contains naturally occurring sugars. When it is added as an ingredient to packaged food, it contributes added sugars on U.S. Nutrition Facts labels.

7 · Fruit-juice concentrate

Concentrated fruit juice can contribute sugars and may be used to sweeten or flavour a product. Its role depends on the recipe and the applicable labelling rules.

8 · Brown rice syrup

A sweet syrup made from rice starch and used as a sweetening ingredient.

A useful distinction: maltodextrin is a starch-derived carbohydrate often used as a bulking agent or flavour carrier. It is not simply another name for table sugar, so consider its role separately.

What to do with this information

Read the full ingredient list rather than judging a product from one word. On U.S. Nutrition Facts labels, use the Added Sugars line to see the declared amount; other countries use different label formats and rules. Compare similar products and consider serving size, price, allergies and your own needs.
