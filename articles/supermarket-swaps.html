Practical supermarket choices

7 supermarket swaps to consider

Small comparisons can make a shop easier. These are flexible examples—not rules, promises of better health, or a list of foods everyone should avoid. Choose what fits your budget, taste, allergies and dietary needs.

  1. Bread: compare the ingredient list

    Try comparing two loaves side by side. Check the first ingredients, whole-grain wording and the nutrition panel; recipes and definitions vary by country.

  2. Breakfast: plain oats or a cereal you enjoy

    Compare serving size, sugars and fibre on similar products. Add fruit or other toppings if they suit you; convenience and preference count too.

  3. Dairy or alternatives: compare unsweetened options

    Plain yoghurt or an unsweetened alternative may be useful to compare with flavoured versions. Check protein, added sugars where listed, calcium fortification and allergens.

  4. Drinks: keep water in the comparison

    Water or sparkling water can be a simple option alongside sweetened drinks. Choose beverages that work for your needs and availability.

  5. Snacks: compare the whole serving

    Plain nuts, popcorn or fruit are examples to consider when suitable. Compare price per portion and check allergens; no single snack fits every person.

  6. Sauces: check sugar and salt per 100 g

    Compare similar sauces using the same basis. A shorter ingredient list is not automatically a nutrition score, and recipes differ.

  7. Quick meals: add a simple base when practical

    Frozen vegetables, rice, eggs or beans can be convenient building blocks where appropriate. Compare cost, preparation time and storage—not just the package front.

Use a comparison, not a verdict. Check the actual package, serving size, full ingredient list and nutrition information. Food-label laws and product formulations differ by market.
