Food-label basics

How to read a food label in 30 seconds

You do not need to memorise every ingredient or spend several minutes on every packet. A consistent first scan can help you compare products more confidently.

Start with the back of the pack

Front-of-pack words and pictures attract attention. Turn the package over and read the ingredient list and nutrition panel for more context.

Read the first three ingredients

Ingredient order gives you a quick picture of the recipe because ingredients are generally listed by weight. The first three are a useful starting point, but they do not replace reading the whole list.

Look for formulation clues

Look for syrups, modified starches, isolated proteins, flavourings, emulsifiers and other ingredients whose role you want to understand. They can tell you more about how a product was formulated and what functions the ingredients perform.

Use the nutrition panel to compare

When comparing similar products, the per-100-g or per-100-ml column makes the comparison less dependent on package size or serving suggestions. Check sugars and salt, and review the other values that matter for the product.

Make a practical choice

Compare like with like. A simpler option may be available in the same aisle, but price, convenience, allergies, dietary needs and personal preferences still matter.

This is a learning method, not a medical diagnosis or a rule that one ingredient makes a food harmful. For the detailed method and full source list, see the Supermarket Decoder guide (on Gumroad).
