Understanding food processing

The four NOVA food-processing levels

“Processed” and “ultra-processed” are not interchangeable. The NOVA framework groups foods according to the nature, extent and purpose of processing.

Group 1 · Unprocessed or minimally processed

Foods such as fresh fruit and vegetables, eggs, plain milk, rice, lentils, fresh meat and fish, and plain nuts. Processing may include cleaning, drying, chilling, freezing or pasteurising.

Group 2 · Processed culinary ingredients

Ingredients obtained from foods and used in cooking, such as olive oil, butter, sugar, salt, flour, honey and vinegar.

Group 3 · Processed foods

Foods made by combining Group 1 foods with Group 2 ingredients, often with relatively simple recipes. Examples can include canned beans, cheese, bakery bread and salted nuts.

Group 4 · Ultra-processed foods

Industrial formulations that commonly contain refined ingredients and additives not typically used in a home kitchen. Examples can include soft drinks, packaged snacks, many breakfast cereals, flavoured yogurts and instant noodles.

What the scale does not tell you

NOVA is a processing classification, not a complete nutritional score. Two foods in the same group can differ considerably in their ingredients and nutritional profile. The same type of food can also sit in different groups depending on its recipe.

Use the processing scale as one lens. Also read the ingredients, nutrition information, portion context and the needs of the person eating the food.
