Additives without the panic

E-numbers explained: understand the additive's job

An E-number is an identifier, not an automatic warning that a food is unhealthy or unsafe.

What does the code tell you?

It identifies a specific additive in systems that use E-number labelling. To understand why it is in a product, look at its technological function: it may help preserve, colour, thicken, regulate acidity or keep ingredients mixed.

E300 — ascorbic acid: vitamin C; used as an antioxidant and in some flour or bakery applications.

E330 — citric acid: acidity regulator; the acid present in citrus fruits is a familiar example.

E440 — pectin: thickening or gelling agent, used in products such as jams.

E500 — sodium bicarbonate: raising agent, commonly known as baking soda.

How to read additive information sensibly

Ask what the additive does, how the overall product is made, and what the rest of the ingredient and nutrition panels show. Some additives are familiar substances; others have specific technical functions. The code alone is not a complete assessment of the food.

Regulatory approval and permitted uses vary by jurisdiction and can change. For legal or safety questions, consult the relevant food-safety authority in your country.
