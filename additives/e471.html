E471: Mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids

E471 is an emulsifier made from fats and glycerol. It keeps bread soft for longer, makes ice cream smoother and stops spreads from separating.

Its jobEmulsifier
Made fromVegetable oils (often palm, soy or rapeseed) or animal fat, reacted with glycerol. The label rarely says which.
On US labels“mono- and diglycerides” or “mono and diglycerides”
Vegan?Not always. It can come from animal fat; look for a vegan label if it matters to you.
Ultra-processing clue?Yes: a NOVA processing clue

Why is E471 added to food?

Fat and water don't mix on their own. E471 has one end that likes fat and one that likes water, so it holds them together. In bread it also slows staling, which is why a packaged loaf stays soft for a week while bakery bread goes hard in two days.

Where you'll find it

  • Packaged sliced bread, buns and wraps
  • Cakes, muffins and croissants
  • Ice cream and whipped toppings
  • Margarine and spreads
  • Instant desserts and some chocolate products

Is E471 safe? What the research says

EFSA re-evaluated E471 in 2017 and found no safety concern at the levels used, without setting a numerical acceptable daily intake. Newer research is looking at emulsifiers more closely: a large French observational study (NutriNet-Santé, BMJ 2023) linked higher intakes of E471 with a higher risk of cardiovascular disease. That shows a link, not proof that E471 causes it, but it's one reason researchers want emulsifiers studied further.

An E-number is an identifier used in the European food-additive numbering system. It does not mean the additive is currently authorised in every country or food category. Rules differ by region and can change, so check local rules. The code is not a danger rating.

Questions people ask about E471

Is E471 vegan?

Not always. E471 can be made from plant oils or animal fat, and labels usually don't say which. Products certified vegan use a plant source.

Does E471 contain trans fats?

It can contain small amounts, depending on the oil it's made from and how it's processed. The amounts you'd get from it in a normal diet are small.

Is E471 a sign of ultra-processed food?

Yes. Emulsifiers are one of the additive types the NOVA system uses as a marker of ultra-processed food.
