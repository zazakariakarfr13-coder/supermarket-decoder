E150d: Sulphite ammonia caramel

E150d is sulphite ammonia caramel, the dark brown colour that makes cola look like cola. It isn't the caramel you'd make at home.

Its jobColour (brown)
Made fromSugar heated with ammonia and sulphite compounds. There are four types of caramel colour, E150a to E150d; E150d is the darkest and most common in drinks.
On US labels“caramel color”
Vegan?Yes.
Ultra-processing clue?Yes: a NOVA processing clue

Why is E150d added to food?

It gives a consistent brown colour, batch after batch, so every bottle of cola, gravy or dark beer looks the same. At the amounts used, it adds almost no taste.

Where you'll find it

  • Colas and dark soft drinks
  • Gravy, sauces and soy sauce
  • Dark beers and some spirits
  • Brown bread and malt loaves
  • Sweets, desserts and pet food

Is E150d safe? What the research says

EFSA set acceptable daily intakes for caramel colours in 2011. The debate is about 4-MEI, a by-product that forms when making E150c and E150d; the WHO's cancer agency (IARC) classes 4-MEI as possibly carcinogenic (group 2B). After California required warnings, cola makers lowered 4-MEI levels in their colour.

Questions people ask about E150d

Is caramel colour the same as caramel?

No. Caramel you make at home is just sugar heated until it browns. Caramel colour is made with chemicals like ammonia or sulphites to get a strong, stable colour.

How can I tell which caramel is used?

Look for the code: E150a is plain caramel, E150b to E150d are made with sulphites, ammonia or both. US labels just say "caramel color".

Is E150d a sign of ultra-processing?

Yes. Colours are one of the additive types NOVA uses as a marker of ultra-processed food.
