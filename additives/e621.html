E621: Monosodium glutamate (MSG)

E621 is monosodium glutamate (MSG), a flavour enhancer that adds a savoury, umami taste. It's the same glutamate found naturally in tomatoes, parmesan and soy sauce.

Vegan?Yes.

Why is E621 added to food?

Glutamate is what makes broth, aged cheese and ripe tomatoes taste deep and savoury. MSG adds that taste cheaply and in a stable form, so crisps, stock cubes and instant noodles taste rich even with simple ingredients.

Where you'll find it

  • Crisps and savoury snacks
  • Stock cubes, gravy and seasonings
  • Instant noodles and soups
  • Ready meals and processed meats
  • Restaurant and takeaway food

Is E621 safe? What the research says

EFSA reviewed glutamates in 2017 and set an acceptable daily intake of 30 mg per kg of body weight, noting that some people's total intake could go above it. The "Chinese restaurant syndrome" (headaches and flushing after MSG) has not held up in controlled, double-blind studies, though a few people report symptoms after large amounts on an empty stomach. MSG has about a third of the sodium of table salt.

An E-number means an additive has been assessed and approved in Europe. It's an ID, not a verdict: what matters is the dose, how often you eat it, and the rest of the recipe. Rules differ between countries and can change.

Questions people ask about E621

Is MSG the same as natural glutamate?

Yes. Your body can't tell the glutamate in MSG from the glutamate in tomatoes or parmesan.

What does "no added MSG" mean?

Only that MSG wasn't added. The product may still contain yeast extract or hydrolysed protein, which are rich in glutamate and do the same job.

Is MSG a sign of ultra-processing?

Yes. Flavour enhancers are one of the additive types NOVA uses as a marker of ultra-processed food.
