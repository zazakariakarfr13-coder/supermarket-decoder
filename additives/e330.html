E330: Citric acid

E330 is citric acid, the same acid found in lemons. It adds a sour taste, controls acidity and helps foods keep their colour and freshness.

Its jobAcidity regulator
Made fromAlmost all of it is made by fermenting sugar with a mould (Aspergillus niger), not squeezed from lemons.
On US labels“citric acid”
Vegan?Yes.
Ultra-processing clue?Not on its own

Why is E330 added to food?

Acidity does several jobs at once: it adds a fresh, sour taste, slows the growth of bacteria, stops cut fruit going brown and helps jam set. That's why citric acid is one of the most used additives in the world.

Where you'll find it

  • Soft drinks and juices
  • Sweets and jellies
  • Jams and canned fruit
  • Canned vegetables and sauces
  • Frozen fruit and many ready meals

Is E330 safe? What the research says

Citric acid is part of how your body makes energy every day, and international food-safety experts (JECFA) haven't set any limit on how much can be eaten. The one thing to know: like any acid, very sour drinks and sweets can wear away tooth enamel if you have them often.

An E-number means an additive has been assessed and approved in Europe. It's an ID, not a verdict: what matters is the dose, how often you eat it, and the rest of the recipe. Rules differ between countries and can change.

Questions people ask about E330

Is citric acid natural?

The molecule is identical to the one in lemons, but the citric acid in food is made by fermenting sugar with a mould. It's natural in origin, made in a factory.

Is citric acid bad for you?

No limit has been set on how much is safe. The main thing to watch is tooth enamel with very sour drinks and sweets.

Is E330 a sign of ultra-processing?

Not on its own. Acidity regulators aren't one of the "cosmetic" additive types the NOVA system uses as a marker.
