E211: Sodium benzoate

E211 is sodium benzoate, a preservative that stops yeasts and moulds growing in acidic foods like soft drinks, sauces and pickles.

Its jobPreservative
Made fromBenzoic acid (found naturally in cranberries and cinnamon) combined with sodium. The food version is made industrially.

Vegan?Yes.

Why is E211 added to food?

Sodium benzoate works best in sour foods, so it's a favourite in soft drinks, squash, ketchup and pickles. It lets an opened bottle last much longer before it ferments or grows mould.

Where you'll find it

  • Soft drinks and squash
  • Ketchup, sauces and dressings
  • Pickles and olives
  • Fruit juices and jams
  • Some margarines

Is E211 safe? What the research says

EFSA reviewed benzoates in 2016 and set an acceptable daily intake of 5 mg per kg of body weight. Two things are worth knowing. With vitamin C (ascorbic acid), heat and light, small amounts of benzene can form in drinks; after US FDA tests in the 2000s, most drinks were found to have very low levels and some were reformulated. And sodium benzoate was part of the additive mix in the 2007 Southampton study on children's hyperactivity.

An E-number means an additive has been assessed and approved in Europe. It's an ID, not a verdict: what matters is the dose, how often you eat it, and the rest of the recipe. Rules differ between countries and can change.

Questions people ask about E211

Can sodium benzoate turn into benzene?

Small amounts can form when it's combined with vitamin C and exposed to heat or light. Tests have found very low levels in most drinks, and many brands reformulated to prevent it.

Is sodium benzoate natural?

Benzoic acid occurs in cranberries and cinnamon, but the sodium benzoate in food is made in a factory.

Is E211 a sign of ultra-processing?

Not on its own. Preservatives aren't one of NOVA's "cosmetic" additive markers.
