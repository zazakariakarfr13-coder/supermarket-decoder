E322: Lecithins

E322 is lecithin, a natural emulsifier usually taken from soy or sunflower. It helps chocolate flow, keeps spreads smooth and stops ingredients separating.

Made fromMostly soybeans or sunflower seeds, sometimes rapeseed or egg yolk. It's a by-product of making oil.

Why is E322 added to food?

Lecithin sits between fat and water and holds them together. In chocolate, a tiny amount makes it flow better, so the maker can use less cocoa butter. In cooking sprays it stops food sticking to the pan.

Where you'll find it

  • Chocolate and chocolate spreads
  • Margarine and spreads
  • Cereal bars and biscuits
  • Cooking sprays
  • Protein powders and instant drinks

Is E322 safe? What the research says

EFSA re-evaluated lecithins in 2017 and found no safety concern at the levels used. Lecithin is also found naturally in egg yolks, soybeans and many other foods.

An E-number is an identifier used in the European food-additive numbering system. It does not mean the additive is currently authorised in every country or food category. Rules differ by region and can change, so check local rules. The code is not a danger rating.

Questions people ask about E322

Is soy lecithin an allergen?

In the EU and UK, soy lecithin must be labelled as soya, one of the 14 allergens. It contains very little soy protein, so many people with a soy allergy tolerate it, but check with your doctor.

Is lecithin bad for you?

No safety concern has been found at the levels used in food. Its presence alone doesn't make a product good or bad; look at the rest of the list.

What's the difference between soy and sunflower lecithin?

They do the same job. Sunflower lecithin is often chosen by brands that want to avoid soy on the label.
