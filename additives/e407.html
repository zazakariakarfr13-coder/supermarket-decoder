E407: Carrageenan

E407 is carrageenan, a thickener and gelling agent extracted from red seaweed. It gives dairy desserts and plant milks a creamy texture and stops cocoa settling in chocolate milk.

Why is E407 added to food?

Carrageenan binds with milk proteins, so a small amount makes a dessert set or a drink feel creamy. In chocolate milk it keeps the cocoa from sinking to the bottom, and in some sliced meats it helps hold water.

Where you'll find it

  • Chocolate milk and flavoured milks
  • Plant milks and vegan cheeses
  • Ice cream, custards and dairy desserts
  • Some sliced meats and ready meals
  • Jellies and vegan desserts

Is E407 safe? What the research says

Carrageenan is approved in the EU. When EFSA re-evaluated it in 2018, it kept a temporary acceptable daily intake and asked for more data, especially for its use in infant formula. Some animal and lab studies, and a few small studies in people with inflammatory bowel disease, suggest it may affect the gut; human evidence is still limited. Food-grade carrageenan is not the same as “degraded carrageenan” (poligeenan), which isn't allowed in food.

An E-number is an identifier used in the European food-additive numbering system. It does not mean the additive is currently authorised in every country or food category. Rules differ by region and can change, so check local rules. The code is not a danger rating.

Questions people ask about E407

Is carrageenan natural?

It comes from seaweed, but it's extracted and purified in a factory before it's added to food.

Why do some people avoid carrageenan?

Because of studies suggesting it may irritate the gut, mostly in animals and in people with inflammatory bowel disease. It's still approved, and EFSA asked for more data in 2018.

Is carrageenan the same as agar?

No. Both come from red seaweed, but agar is E406 and sets into a firmer gel.
