E250: Sodium nitrite

E250 is sodium nitrite, the preservative that cures ham and bacon. It stops dangerous bacteria, keeps the meat pink and gives it its cured taste.

Its jobPreservative
Made fromA mineral salt made industrially. It's usually mixed with salt as “curing salt”.
On US labels“sodium nitrite”
Vegan?Yes, though it's used almost only in meat.
Ultra-processing clue?Not on its own

Why is E250 added to food?

Nitrite protects cured meats against Clostridium botulinum, the bacterium behind botulism. It also reacts with the meat's pigment, which is why ham is pink instead of grey, and it gives bacon its typical flavour.

Where you'll find it

  • Ham, bacon and gammon
  • Salami, chorizo and pepperoni
  • Hot dogs and frankfurters
  • Corned beef and some pâtés
  • Some smoked fish

Is E250 safe? What the research says

Nitrites can form nitrosamines, many of which can cause cancer, especially when cured meat is cooked at high heat, like crispy bacon. They're one reason the WHO's cancer agency (IARC) classed processed meat as carcinogenic (group 1) in 2015, mainly for bowel cancer. In 2023 the EU lowered the maximum amounts allowed in meat products. Most health bodies advise eating processed meat only now and then.

Questions people ask about E250

Is "uncured" or "no added nitrite" ham nitrite-free?

Often not. Many of these products use celery powder or juice, which is naturally rich in nitrate that turns into nitrite during curing. The result can contain similar nitrite levels.

Why is vitamin C often added to ham?

Sodium ascorbate (E301) speeds up curing and reduces the formation of nitrosamines.

Does E250 make a food ultra-processed?

Preservatives aren't one of NOVA's "cosmetic" markers, but many processed meats contain other markers, like dextrose or flavourings.
