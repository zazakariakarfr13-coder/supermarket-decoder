E129: Allura red AC (Red 40)

E129 is Allura red AC, called Red 40 in the US. It's a synthetic red dye used in sweets, drinks and snacks, and in the EU it carries a warning about children's attention.

Its jobColour (red)
Made fromA synthetic “azo” dye made from petroleum-derived chemicals.
On US labels“Red 40”, “FD&C Red No. 40” or “Red 40 Lake”
Vegan?Yes (unlike carmine, E120, which comes from insects).
Ultra-processing clue?Yes: a NOVA processing clue

Why is E129 added to food?

It gives a strong, bright red that stays stable in light and heat and costs little, so sweets, drinks and cereals look exciting. It does nothing for taste or freshness.

Where you'll find it

  • Sweets, gummies and lollipops
  • Soft drinks, sports drinks and squash
  • Breakfast cereals and snacks
  • Desserts, icing and jelly
  • Some medicines

Is E129 safe? What the research says

E129 is one of the six "Southampton colours". After a 2007 study linked mixtures of these colours with hyperactivity in some children, the EU and UK required foods containing them to carry the warning "may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children". Many European brands switched to colours like beetroot or paprika. In 2025 the US FDA announced a plan to phase out petroleum-based dyes like Red 40, which depends largely on manufacturers acting.

An E-number is an identifier used in the European food-additive numbering system. It does not mean the additive is currently authorised in every country or food category. Rules differ by region and can change, so check local rules. The code is not a danger rating.

Questions people ask about E129

Is Red 40 banned in Europe?

No. It's allowed, but foods containing it must carry a warning about children's activity and attention, so many brands use other colours instead.

Why do US and EU versions of the same sweet look different?

Many European versions use colouring foods like beetroot, paprika or spirulina instead of synthetic dyes, partly because of the warning label.

Is E129 a sign of ultra-processing?

Yes. Colours are one of the additive types NOVA uses as a marker of ultra-processed food.
