E415: Xanthan gum

E415 is xanthan gum, a thickener made by bacteria fermenting sugar. A pinch makes sauces thick and dressings stable, and it holds gluten-free bread together.

Why is E415 added to food?

Xanthan gum is extremely strong: about 0.1 to 0.5% is enough to thicken a sauce. It stays thick when hot or cold and stops ingredients separating, so a salad dressing doesn't split in the bottle. In gluten-free baking it partly replaces the stretch that gluten gives.

Where you'll find it

  • Salad dressings and sauces
  • Gluten-free bread and baking mixes
  • Ice cream and dairy desserts
  • Ready meals and soups
  • Plant milks and smoothies

Is E415 safe? What the research says

EFSA re-evaluated xanthan gum in 2017 and found no safety concern for the general population at the levels used. Like other fibres, eaten in large amounts it can cause bloating or a laxative effect.

An E-number means an additive has been assessed and approved in Europe. It's an ID, not a verdict: what matters is the dose, how often you eat it, and the rest of the recipe. Rules differ between countries and can change.

Questions people ask about E415

Is xanthan gum gluten-free?

Yes. Even when the sugar it's made from comes from wheat, the finished gum is purified and is used widely in gluten-free baking.

Is xanthan gum a fibre?

Yes, it's a soluble fibre your body doesn't digest. That's why large amounts can cause bloating.

Is E415 a sign of ultra-processing?

Yes. Thickeners and stabilisers are one of the additive types NOVA uses as a marker of ultra-processed food.
