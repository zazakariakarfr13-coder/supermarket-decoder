E171: Titanium dioxide

E171 is titanium dioxide, a white colour that makes sweets, icing and chewing gum bright and opaque. It has been banned as a food additive in the EU since 2022.

Why is E171 added to food?

Titanium dioxide makes colours brighter and covers other colours completely, so a sweet coating looks pure white or a coloured candy looks vivid. It does nothing for taste or freshness.

Where you'll find it

  • Sweets, mints and chewing gum
  • Cake icing and decorations
  • Coated chocolates and candies
  • Some sauces, dressings and coffee creamers
  • Coated tablets and supplements

Is E171 safe? What the research says

In 2021 EFSA concluded that titanium dioxide could no longer be considered safe as a food additive, because concerns that it could damage DNA couldn't be ruled out. The EU banned it in food from 2022, after France had already banned it in 2020. It's still allowed in the UK, the US and many other countries.

An E-number is an identifier used in the European food-additive numbering system. It does not mean the additive is currently authorised in every country or food category. Rules differ by region and can change, so check local rules. The code is not a danger rating.

Questions people ask about E171

Is titanium dioxide banned?

In the EU, yes, since 2022. In the UK and the US it's still allowed in food.

Why does a product sold in the EU still list titanium dioxide?

It might be a medicine or supplement, where different rules apply, or an imported product. In food, it should no longer appear in the EU.

Is E171 a sign of ultra-processing?

Yes. Colours are one of the additive types NOVA uses as a marker of ultra-processed food.
