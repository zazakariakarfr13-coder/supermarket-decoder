One bold package. One curious shopper. Six tiny clues. And a movie night that gets saved by the humble act of turning a packet around.

Case file 07 · Snack aisle mystery
Characters and package shown are fictional. The label examples below are teaching props, not real products.
Previously, in the snack aisle…

Maya came in for one snack. She left with a new superpower.

Not X-ray vision. Not the ability to hear a crisp packet open from three rooms away. Just a simple way to read the information already printed on a food label.

Scene 01 · The opening claim

“This snack looks like it has a personal trainer.”

Maya“Natural! Real fruit! Power bite! It practically came with a motivational podcast.”
Nico“The front is doing a lot of talking. Let's hear the other side.”

Maya turns the packet around. The investigation begins.

Scene 02 · Read the recipe

The first few ingredients tell a story.

INGREDIENTS
  1. Rolled oats
  2. Dates
  3. Peanut butter
  4. Cocoa
  5. Honey
  6. Sea salt
Nico“In the U.S., ingredients are generally listed from most to least by weight. Start with the first few, then read the whole list.”
Clue: the order helps you understand what makes up more of this recipe. It is not a complete nutrition score.
Scene 03 · The name game

Sugar has more than one name.

Some ingredient names you may spot include:

HoneyCane sugarGlucose syrupDextrose
Maya“Wait. Honey is a sweetener too?”
Nico“Exactly. Use the ingredient list as a clue, then check Total Sugars and Includes Added Sugars on the Nutrition Facts label.”

The goal is not to panic at a word. It is to understand the full picture.

Scene 04 · The serving-size plot twist

“I thought the whole pouch was one serving!”

Nutrition Facts
2 servings per container
Serving size1 bar (40g)
Amount per serving
Calories170
Total Sugars10g
Includes Added Sugars6g
Fictional example for learning — not a real product.
Nico“Nutrition Facts numbers are shown per serving. Check how many servings are in the pack—and how much you're actually eating.”
Quick % Daily Value guide: 5% DV or less is low; 20% DV or more is high for a nutrient.
Scene 05 · The additive courtroom

An E-number is an ID—not a verdict.

Maya“E-number? Is this ingredient on trial?”
Nico“Not at all. An E-number identifies an additive. Ask what job it does.”
PreserveColourThickenRegulate acidityHelp ingredients mix

Different places may display additive names and codes differently. The useful question is what the ingredient does—not whether the code looks mysterious.

Scene 06 · The good ending

No food panic. Just a smarter choice.

Maya“So the label didn't tell me what to think. It gave me better information to decide.”
Nico“Exactly. Compare similar products, check the serving size, read the ingredients, then think about your taste, needs and budget.”

Maya picks the snack she actually wants. Nico gets the popcorn. The movie starts on time.

Crumb, the tiny snack mascot: “A happy ending. And not one dramatic courtroom appeal.”

The takeaway · Keep this little checklist

Four clues. One calmer supermarket trip.

You don't need to memorise every ingredient or judge a food by one word. Use the label to ask better questions.

01 · IngredientsStart with the first few. Read the full list.
02 · SugarsNotice sweetener names; check Total and Added Sugars.
03 · ServingCheck the serving size and servings per container.
04 · ContextAsk what an additive does. Compare like with like.
Quick detective test

One bar = one serving. You eat two bars. How many servings is that?

No trick question. Crumb promises not to object.
